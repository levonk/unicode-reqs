## IV. Selection factors — Inclusion

### A. Multiple meanings

The tail conveys several distinct concepts:

1. **Animal body part:** The tail is a fundamental anatomical feature of most
   vertebrates and many invertebrates — used for balance, locomotion, defense,
   social signaling, and mating displays.
2. **Emotion / mood (metaphor):** A wagging tail universally signals happiness or
   excitement (especially for dogs); a tucked tail signals fear or submission.
3. **Following / trailing:** "Tail" means to follow closely ("tail someone," "on
   someone's tail"), a common idiom.
4. **End / rear:** "Tail end" means the last part of something (tail of a
   distribution, tail of a line, tail of a comet).
5. **Fashion:** "Tail" refers to the tail of a coat or tuxedo (formal wear).

### B. Use in sequences

- 🐶🫠 — happy dog wagging tail (dog + tail)
- 🐱🫠 — cat with tail
- 🫠😍 — wagging tail excitement
- 🫠🏃 — tailing someone (following)
- 🫠📉 — long tail (statistics/business)

### C. Breaks new ground

**Yes.** There is no existing emoji for a standalone tail. While many animal emoji
(🐶 dog, 🐱 cat, 🐎 horse, 🦊 fox) include tails as part of the full animal, there
is no emoji for a tail as a body part — analogous to how 🐽 pig nose (U+1F43D) is a
standalone body part, 🐾 paw prints (U+1F43E) is a standalone trace, and 🦴 bone
(U+1F9B4) is a standalone skeletal element.

A standalone tail fills a gap in the "animal body part" subcategory and enables
expressions (mood, following, "long tail") that full-animal emoji cannot convey.

### D. Image distinctiveness

The tail emoji depicts a curved, furry appendage rising from a base, resembling a
generic mammalian tail (e.g., dog or cat tail). At 18×18 pixels, the curved shape
with a fluffy tip is recognizable. It is distinct from 🐾 paw prints (foot prints),
🐽 pig nose (snout), and 🦴 bone (skeletal), as it depicts a flexible, curved
appendage.

## V. Selection factors — Exclusion

### A. Already representable

The tail is **not** already representable as a standalone concept:

- 🐶 dog, 🐱 cat, 🐎 horse, etc. include tails as part of the full animal, but
  cannot convey "tail" as a concept (mood, following, "long tail") independently.
- 🐾 paw prints represents a trace, not a body part.
- 🐽 pig nose is a different body part (snout).
- No sequence of existing emoji conveys "a standalone tail."

### B. Overly specific

No. "Tail" is a general, universal anatomical feature, not a specific species'
tail. The emoji represents tails in general (mammalian, reptilian, avian), not one
specific animal's tail. This is analogous to 🐽 pig nose representing snouts/noses
in general, and 🦴 bone representing bones in general.

### C. Open-ended

No. A tail is a single, well-defined body part. Adding it does not create pressure
to add "scales," "claws," "antlers," or other body parts — those are distinct
concepts. The tail is one of the most iconic and universally recognized animal
features, comparable to 🐾 paw prints and 🦴 bone which were already encoded.

### D. Transient

No. The tail has been a recognized anatomical term and cultural symbol for
centuries. Its usage in biology, pet culture, fashion, and idioms is stable and
not faddish.

### E. Faulty comparison

This proposal is not justified by similarity to existing emoji. It stands on its
own frequency and distinctiveness evidence. The existence of 🐽 pig nose or 🐾 paw
prints does not justify this proposal; rather, the tail fills a distinct gap as a
standalone animal body part.

## VI. Other information

### Design considerations

- The emoji should depict a generic curved tail (mammalian style) with a slightly
  fluffy/furred tip, recognizable across species.
- A tan/brown color scheme allows it to represent a range of animals.
- At 18×18 pixels, the key distinguishing features are: curved appendage rising
  from a base, with a slightly wider/fluffy tip.
- The design should be distinct from 🐾 paw prints (footprints) and 🦴 bone
  (skeletal element).

### Sort location

Animals & Nature → animal-mammal, placed near 🐽 pig nose (U+1F43D), 🐾 paw prints
(U+1F43E), and 🦴 bone (U+1F9B4), as it is a standalone animal body part.
